## 1–4. Benchmark tables (Foundations, Reading, Vocabulary, Communication)

"Clarification" notes are quoted or condensed from the official benchmark clarifications. The "How the app should practice it" column contains design recommendations. They are not part of the standard.

### 1a. Foundations: ELA.1.F.1 Learning and Applying Foundational Reading Skills

| Code | Exact benchmark text | How the app should practice it |
|---|---|---|
| **ELA.1.F.1.1** (Print Concepts) | Locate the title, table of contents, names of author(s) and illustrator(s), and glossary of books. | Use a mini-book cover and front-matter screens with tap-to-find prompts ("Tap the title," "Who drew the pictures?"). Add a simple table of contents that jumps to a page, and a 3–5 word glossary page. |
| **ELA.1.F.1.2** (Phonological Awareness) | Demonstrate phonological awareness. | **Audio only, no letters on screen.** Clarification 1: "Phonological awareness only refers to what can be done orally at both the sound and syllabic level... It does not involve print or letter knowledge." |
| ELA.1.F.1.2.a | Segment spoken words into initial, medial, and final phonemes, including words with digraphs, blends, and trigraphs. | Play a word such as "ship." The child taps one sound box per phoneme (/sh/ /i/ /p/). Include blends (*stop*) and trigraphs (*catch*). |
| ELA.1.F.1.2.b | Orally blend initial, medial, and final phonemes together to produce a single-syllable word that includes digraphs, blends, or trigraphs. | Play separate phonemes, then the child picks the matching picture or says the word (speech recognition is optional). |
| ELA.1.F.1.2.c | Blend single-syllable spoken words with at least five phonemes. | Use 5-phoneme words (*stamp*, *plant*, *crust*, *blend*) for "robot talk" blending. |
| ELA.1.F.1.2.d | Segment single-syllable spoken words with at least five phonemes. | Five-box Elkonin segmenting with audio-only prompts. |
| ELA.1.F.1.2.e | Segment and blend phonemes in multi-syllable spoken words. | Two-syllable spoken words (*sunset*, *rabbit*): first segment the syllables, then the phonemes. |
| **ELA.1.F.1.3** (Phonics and Word Analysis) | Use knowledge of grade-appropriate phonics and word-analysis skills to decode words accurately. | Systematic phonics scope and sequence. Practice uses decodable words and decodable sentences only. **Never prompt "guess from the picture"** (see section 6, three-cueing law). |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.a | Decode words using knowledge of spelling-sound correspondences for common consonant digraphs, trigraphs, and blends. | Digraphs sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng. Trigraphs tch, dge. Blends: initial (st, bl, tr...) and final (-mp, -nd, -st). Tap-to-sound each grapheme, then read the whole word. |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.b | Decode simple words with r-controlled vowels. | ar, or, er/ir/ur word sets (*car*, *fork*, *her*, *bird*, *turn*). |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.c | Decode and encode regularly spelled one-syllable words. | **Includes spelling (encoding).** Dictation mode: play a word, the child builds it with letter tiles or types it. Pair each decoding set with an encoding set. |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.d | Decode words with inflectional endings. | -s, -es, -ed (three sounds: /t/ /d/ /ed/), -ing, on known base words. Ties to ELA.1.V.1.2. |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.e | Decode two-syllable words with regular patterns by breaking the words into syllables. | Closed-syllable compound and VC/CV words (*sunset*, *napkin*, *rabbit*). Show a visual syllable split, read each part, then read the whole word. |
| ELA.1.F.1.3.f | Decode words that use final –e and vowel teams to make long-vowel sound. | VCe (*cake*, *bike*, *home*, *cute*). Vowel teams ai/ay, ee/ea, oa/ow, igh. Contrast pairs (*cap*/*cape*). |
| **ELA.1.F.1.4** (Fluency) | Read grade-level texts with accuracy, automaticity, and appropriate prosody or expression. | Timed one-minute passage reads with WCPM tracking (see section 1e norms), then a comprehension check. Clarification 4: prosody means "pausing patterns during oral reading that reflect the punctuation and meaning of a text." Add an optional NAEP-style 1–4 phrasing rating by the parent. |
| ELA.1.F.1.4.a | Recognize and read with automaticity the grade-level sight words. | Flash-card speed drills on Dolch and Fry words (section 5). Per Clarification 2, decodable high-frequency words should be taught with their phonics pattern. Irregular ("heart") words get extra repetition. |

Official clarifications for F.1.3 and F.1.4 that matter for the app:
- F.1.3, Clarification 2: "Students will decode decodable high frequency words appropriate to the grade level. See 1.F.1.4 and Dolch and Fry word lists. Students will read grade-level appropriate high frequency words, decodable or not, with automaticity."
- F.1.4, Clarification 3: "See Fluency Norms for grade-level norms. Norms are expressed as words correct per minute (WCPM), a measure that combines accuracy with speed."
- F.1.4, Clarification 5: "Grade-level texts, for the purposes of fluency, are those within the grade band on quantitative text complexity measures and appropriate in content and qualitative measures."

### 1b. Reading: ELA.1.R (benchmarks for plot, story elements, central message, topic and details, compare/contrast, text features)

| Code | Exact benchmark text | How the app should practice it |
|---|---|---|
| **ELA.1.R.1.1** (Literary Elements) | Identify and describe the main story elements in a story. | Questions after each story: Who? Where? What happened first, next, last? Picture-card sequencing (drag 3–4 events into order). Clarification 1 defines the elements as "the setting, characters, and sequence of events." Clarification 3: address the time element of setting "only... where it is explicitly indicated." **This is where sequence of events and plot live in grade 1.** |
| **ELA.1.R.1.2** (Theme) | Identify and explain the moral of a story. | "What lesson did ___ learn?" multiple choice plus a "why?" follow-up. Clarification 1: this is the precursor to theme. Include some stories without a moral. **This is the grade-1 "central message."** B.E.S.T. calls it "moral"; the word "central message" does not appear in grade 1. |
| ELA.1.R.1.3 (Perspective and Point of View) | Explain who is telling the story using context clues. | "Who is telling this story?" Choose a character (I/me text) or a narrator outside the story, and point to the clue words. |
| ELA.1.R.1.4 (Poetry) | Identify stanzas and line breaks in poems. | Short poems: tap where a stanza ends, count the stanzas. |
| **ELA.1.R.2.1** (Structure) | Use text features including titles, headings, captions, graphs, maps, glossaries, and/or illustrations to demonstrate understanding of texts. | Informational mini-texts with headings, captions, a simple bar graph or map, and a glossary. Questions are answered from the feature ("Look at the caption. What is the frog doing?"). |
| **ELA.1.R.2.2** (Central Idea) | Identify the topic of and relevant details in a text. | "What is this text mostly about?" (topic), then "Pick 2 details that tell about the topic," with distractor details. **Grade-1 version of "main topic and key details."** |
| ELA.1.R.2.3 (Purpose and Perspective) | Explain similarities and differences between information provided in visuals and words in an informational text. | "What does the picture show that the words don't?" Venn or T-chart drag-sort (Clarification 2 names these tools). |
| ELA.1.R.2.4 (Argument) | Identify an author's opinion(s) about the topic. | Short opinion texts: "What does the author think about ___?" |
| ELA.1.R.3.1 (Interpreting Figurative Language) | Identify and explain descriptive words and phrases in text(s). | Tap the describing words (adjectives) and explain what they add. |
| **ELA.1.R.3.2** (Paraphrasing and Summarizing) | Retell a text in oral or written form to enhance comprehension. | Record-a-retell (audio) or a guided retell with beginning/middle/end picture prompts. |
| ELA.1.R.3.2.a | Use main story elements at the beginning, middle, and end for a literary text. | Beginning/middle/end retell frames for stories. |
| ELA.1.R.3.2.b | Use topic and important details for an informational text. | "Topic + 2 details" retell frame for informational texts. |
| **ELA.1.R.3.3** (Comparative Reading) | Compare and contrast two texts on the same topic. | Paired passages on one topic (two texts about frogs). Venn diagram drag-sort of same and different. **Note:** grade 1 compares two **texts**, not two characters. Comparing characters is not a separate grade-1 benchmark (UNVERIFIED that it appears anywhere else in grade 1; I found none). |

### 1c. Vocabulary: ELA.1.V.1 Finding Meaning

| Code | Exact benchmark text | How the app should practice it |
|---|---|---|
| **ELA.1.V.1.1** (Academic Vocabulary) | Use grade-level academic vocabulary appropriately in speaking and writing. | Pre-teach 2–3 tier-2 or academic words per story with child-friendly definitions, then reuse them in questions. (SDPBC paraphrase: "Recognize and appropriately use..." Use the FLDOE wording.) |
| **ELA.1.V.1.2** (Morphology) | Identify and use frequently occurring base words and their common inflections in grade-level content. | Base word + ending builder (*jump* → *jumps*, *jumped*, *jumping*). The official grade-1 base-word list (Appendix D) is: close, do, give, happy, help, jump, kind, like, open, pack, play, ride, talk, teach, walk. |
| **ELA.1.V.1.3** (Context and Connotation) | Identify and use picture clues, context clues, word relationships, reference materials, and/or background knowledge to determine the meaning of unknown words. | Applies to **word meaning**, never to word reading or decoding. Read-aloud vocabulary questions (Clarification 1: read-alouds "can be two grade levels higher than student reading level"). Synonym and antonym matching. A picture glossary as the reference material. |

Appendix D context-clue types: Definition, Synonyms, Antonyms, Examples, Inference. Word relationships: Synonym, Antonym, Homonym, Homophone (e.g., there/their, to/too, here/hear).

### 1d. Communication: handwriting, conventions, spelling

| Code | Exact benchmark text | How the app should practice it |
|---|---|---|
| **ELA.1.C.1.1** (Handwriting) | Print all upper- and lowercase letters. | Optional finger or stylus letter tracing. Clarification 1: "Students should have adequate spacing between letters and/or words." Low priority for a reading app. |
| ELA.1.C.1.2 (Narrative Writing) | Write narratives that retell two or more appropriately sequenced events, including relevant details and a sense of closure. | Optional: "Write or tell what happened first and next" after a story. |
| **ELA.1.C.3.1** (Conventions) | Follow the rules of standard English grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and spelling appropriate to grade level. | **This is the only Communication benchmark that names spelling.** Practice capitals and end punctuation in sentence-fix games, plus dictation. Encoding also lives in ELA.1.F.1.3.c. |

ELA.1.C.3.1, Clarification 1, skills to be **mastered** in grade 1: Capitalize proper nouns. Form and use simple verb tenses for regular verbs by adding the affix -ed. Form and use complete simple sentences. Use possessives. Use subject-verb agreement in simple sentences.

Skills **introduced but not yet mastered**: Form plurals -y to -ies. Conjugate regular and irregular verb tenses. Form and use regular and frequently occurring irregular plural nouns. Form and use the past tense of frequently occurring irregular verbs. Use apostrophes to form contractions. Appropriately use pronouns. Use commas in a series. Use plural possessives. Use interjections.

Kindergarten skills marked "R" (review) in grade 1, per the Conventions Progression in Appendix C: Begin each sentence with a capital letter and use ending punctuation. Capitalize the days of the week, the months of the year, and the pronoun I. Form regular plural nouns orally by adding /s/ or /es/. Use interrogatives to ask questions.

Other grade-1 Communication codes (not core to the app): ELA.1.C.1.3 (opinion writing), ELA.1.C.1.4 (expository writing), ELA.1.C.1.5 (improving writing), ELA.1.C.2.1 (oral presentation), ELA.1.C.4.1 (research), ELA.1.C.5.1 (multimedia), ELA.1.C.5.2 (digital tools).

Cross-cutting expectations (K-12): ELA.K12.EE.1.1 "Cite evidence to explain and justify reasoning." ELA.K12.EE.2.1 "Read and comprehend grade-level complex texts proficiently." ELA.K12.EE.3.1 "Make inferences to support comprehension." The app's "How do you know? Point to the words" follow-ups align with EE.1.1.

### 1e. Grade-1 oral reading fluency norms (official B.E.S.T. Appendix E)

B.E.S.T. Appendix E, "Measures of Fluency / Fluency Norms" (guide p. 209), reproduces Hasbrouck, J. & Tindal, G. (2017), *An update on compiled ORF norms* (Technical Report No. 1702), University of Oregon. Grade 1, words correct per minute (WCPM):

| Percentile | Fall WCPM | Winter WCPM | Spring WCPM |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | (none) | 97 | 116 |
| 75 | (none) | 59 | 91 |
| **50** | (none) | **29** | **60** |
| 25 | (none) | 16 | 34 |
| 10 | (none) | 9 | 18 |

There is no fall norm for grade 1. Appendix E also says that fluency measures "should always be combined with comprehension checks such as a retelling of the passage or comprehension questions." It also includes the NAEP 4-level Fluency Scale (Levels 3–4 fluent, 1–2 non-fluent) as a sample prosody rubric.

App suggestion: treat the 50th percentile (winter 29, spring 60 WCPM) as the on-track target line, and track accuracy separately (WCPM combines both). These norms are for cold reads of grade-level passages.

**Florida has no list of its own.** The B.E.S.T. ## 6. Florida law on three-cueing, and FAST K-2 (Star) grade-1 cut scores

### Three-cueing (Florida law)

- **Law:** CS/CS/HB 7039 (2023), "Student Outcomes," **Chapter 2023-108, Laws of Florida**. Approved by the Governor May 17, 2023; effective July 1, 2023.
- **Key statutes:**
  - **s. 1001.215(7), F.S.** (Just Read, Florida! Office): "The primary instructional strategy for teaching word reading is phonics instruction for decoding and encoding." Reading programs "may not include strategies that employ the three-cueing system model of reading or visual memory as a basis for teaching word reading." They "may include visual information and strategies that improve background and experiential knowledge, add context, and increase oral language and vocabulary to support comprehension, but may not be used to teach word reading."
  - **s. 1008.25(8)(a), F.S.**: "Instructional strategies may not employ the three-cueing system model of reading or visual memory as a basis for teaching word reading." This sits in the subsection on retained third-grade students.
  - Per the House staff analysis, the bill also amended ss. 1001.42, 1002.20, 1002.33, 1002.411, 1002.59, 1006.31, 1008.365, 1011.62, 1012.56, 1012.585, and 1012.98 (instructional materials, teacher preparation, professional learning, charter schools, and others).
- **Scope:** the ban is **system-wide** (all reading instruction, materials, teacher preparation and training), **not limited to K-3**. K-3 reading instruction and intervention fall squarely under it.
- **One-sentence summary:** since July 1, 2023, Florida law requires phonics (decoding and encoding) as the primary strategy for teaching word reading and bans the three-cueing (meaning/structure/visual, MSV) model and visual-memory approaches as a basis for teaching word reading. Pictures and context may support comprehension and vocabulary only.
- **App implication:** decoding prompts must say "sound it out" or "look at all the letters." Never "look at the picture," "what would make sense?" or "skip it and come back" as word-identification strategies. Picture clues are allowed only for word-meaning tasks (ELA.1.V.1.3) and comprehension.

### FAST K-2 (Renaissance Star) grade-1 cut scores

FAST K-2 ELA Reading uses Renaissance **Star Early Literacy and/or Star Reading** (FLDOE wording) and is given three times a year: PM1 (beginning of year), PM2 (mid-year), PM3 (end of year).

The official cut scores were adopted by the State Board of Education on **October 18, 2023** in **Rule 6A-1.09422, F.A.C.** They are reported from fall 2023–24 onward. Grade 1 ELA Reading:

| Level | Star Unified Scale | FAST (B.E.S.T.) equivalent scale |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | below 791 (600–790) | 0–134 |
| Level 2 | 791–843 | 135–152 |
| **Level 3 (on grade level)** | **844–893** | **153–169** |
| Level 4 | 894–946 | 170–187 |
| Level 5 | 947 and above (947–1400) | 188–341 |

- **Important:** these are **static, end-of-year (PM3) benchmarks**. FLDOE says: "State benchmarks presented in the Star Student Report and Family Portal are static, not time-adjusted, meaning they are compared to end-of-school-year expectations." It also says students "may not yet be at grade level on the B.E.S.T. scale for the PM1 and PM2 administrations, but this does not necessarily indicate that a student is not on track."
- **There are no separate official fall or winter Level-3 cut scores.** Renaissance may show district or time-adjusted benchmarks inside Star reports, but I found no official FLDOE or Renaissance publication of fall or winter grade-1 Level-3 Unified cuts. **UNVERIFIED / not published.**
- For context only, the superseded 2022–23 FSA-linked spring cuts for grade 1 were: Level 3 = 839 Unified (46th percentile), Level 2 = 788, Level 4 = 887, Level 5 = 944. These are **no longer in use**.
- App implication: the app should not claim to predict FAST scores. At most, show the parent the official Level-3 range (844–893 Unified at PM3) next to the child's school-reported score.
